A market that learns to read itself.

Two hundred traders evolve. They build habits from what paid them, and shadow habits they never chose. A Mind watches only the chart, tests eight theories of markets against what actually happens, discovers its own map of market moods with no labels, and publishes its forecast back to the crowd. Then the crowd trades on it, and the Mind has to understand a market that now contains itself.

The eight theories, strung into one puzzle

How it works

  • Economy: value grows with the productivity of the evolutionary economy (firms mutate, compete, die). A hidden chain of regimes shocks it: expansion, euphoria, crisis, stagflation, recovery. Calm makes euphoria likely, and euphoria makes crisis likely.
  • Crowd: 200 traders. Each has a genome over five strategies, its own pattern memory, habits that reinforce on profit, and three shadow habits that build up from experience (big losses leave scars, winning with the herd breeds conformity, dip-buys that worked become reflex). Every quarter the poorest 5% die and the richest reproduce with mutation. Shadows pass to the next generation at half strength.
  • Mind: sees price, order flow and noisy earnings. Each theory fits its own coefficient online and is scored on 5-day outcomes. It models the crowd's flow by recursive least squares, and the coefficients on drawdown and herd flow are its estimate of the shadow. It clusters market states into archetypes with no labels, and keeps separate theory weights for each archetype.
  • Loop: the Mind's forecast is published, and traders with a "follow the Mind" gene trade on it. The model is part of the market it's modeling.

What this is not

  • Not sentient. It is a self-model: a system that keeps a running account of what it is and corrects it with evidence. That's the honest version of "a market that understands itself."
  • Not a trading signal. A stylized, daily, synthetic market. Every assumption is a parameter you can change in market.js.
  • Skill is noisy. Some histories teach the Mind a lot. In others, the feedback loop keeps eating its edge. That's the point.

Falsifiers: what would prove the idea wrong

  • If the Mind's archetypes matched the hidden regimes no better than chance (calling every day the most common regime, shown under the tile), it isn't discovering structure.
  • If skill never rises above 0 across seeds, eight theories plus memory is no better than "no change."
  • If its shadow estimate doesn't track the hidden shadow flow, biases can't be read from the tape.
  • If turning the feedback loop up never lowers skill, reflexivity doesn't matter here.
