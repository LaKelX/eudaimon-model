Seven readings of the map

I · THE MAP HAS A THESIS

Radzicki was a system-dynamics evolutionary economist arguing for his own field, so the drawing argues too. The left side (Chicago, Lucas, New Classical, Supply Side) ends in dead-end boxes with no arrows to the future. The bottom-right ovals point at each other in a dense web. It's a family tree drawn by the side that thinks it won. Read it as a claim, not a census.

II · BIOLOGY CAME IN THROUGH ONE DOOR

Malthus → Darwin → Veblen. Malthus's population pressure gave Darwin natural selection. Veblen carried selection back into economics in 1898 and asked why economics wasn't an evolutionary science. Every oval on the right descends from that one loop out of economics and back. Evolutionary economics is economics that spent a century in biology and came home.

III · PHYSICS CAME IN THROUGH THREE

Von Neumann (game theory, computation), Wiener (cybernetics, feedback) and Prigogine (thermodynamics, self-organization), with Georgescu-Roegen's entropy law feeding ecological economics. "You can't argue with thermodynamics" is not a slogan. It's a Georgescu-Roegen inheritance.

IV · FOUR HUBS CARRY THE WEIGHT

Smith starts everything, Marx splits it, Keynes is the busiest crossroads, and Schumpeter is the main source for the modern schools. Cut Keynes and the middle of the map falls apart. Cut Schumpeter and evolutionary economics loses its main parent. Hayek is the odd hub: claimed by the right through Chicago and the Austrians, and by the evolutionists through spontaneous order.

V · THE SEVEN OVALS ARE ONE BLOC

Institutional, behavioral, evolutionary, post-Keynesian, ecological, agent-based and radical political economy all point at each other. They're not seven fields. They're one rebellion against a single assumption: the rational, representative agent at equilibrium. What they share is history, heterogeneous agents, bounded minds, power and limits.

VI · THE REBELS WON BY BEING ABSORBED

Since 2003 the mainstream ate the bloc. Kahneman (2002) and Thaler (2017) won Nobels for behavioral economics. Ostrom won for institutions (2009). Central banks now run models with heterogeneous agents, and after 2008 everyone quotes Minsky. The dead-end boxes on the left didn't die. They mutated, which is evolutionary economics proving itself on its own discipline.

VII · THE OPEN SEAT IS THE SYNTHESIS

No node on the map joins all seven ovals in one working system. Each school owns a piece: habits, routines, demand, fragility, entropy, ownership, agents. The unclaimed position is the model that runs them together and lets them be tested against each other. That is the seat Eudaimon is built for.

What the 2003 map leaves out

Missing people

Kahneman & Tversky (loss aversion) aren't on the map, a year after the Nobel. Ostrom (governing the commons). Polanyi (markets embedded in society). Penrose (how firms grow). Sen (capability and justice). Soros (reflexivity). Graham and Fama, the poles of value and efficient markets. And Ibn Khaldun, who wrote on the division of labor, tax cycles and the rise and fall of dynasties 400 years before Smith.

Missing since 2003

Piketty (r > g), Modern Monetary Theory, mechanism and market design, network and complexity economics, Mazzucato's entrepreneurial state, and the economics of AI and automation, which is the question this whole lineage now has to answer.

Who isn't in the room

One woman, Joan Robinson, among about 90 names. Nobody from the Global South. No one who studied the economy from the bottom up, from the position of those who own nothing. The map is a record of who got to write, not of who had the ideas.

The pedigree line

“My work descends from Schumpeter’s creative destruction, Veblen’s habits, Minsky’s fragility and Hayek’s knowledge problem, computed one agent at a time. Ricardo showed that scarce land collects the rent. I show who collects it when the scarce thing is a chip, a power line or a water right.”

That's how you introduce yourself to an economist: a lineage they recognize, plus the one question it hasn't answered.
