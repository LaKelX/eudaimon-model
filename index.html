Three economies. Same luck. Different owners.

Firms carry routines like genes: they mutate through R&D, copy the best, and die when the market selects against them. Households earn wages and own capital, or don't. AI eats power, and power runs through a grid where an old regime fights a new technology. Three economies run on the same random seed: the status quo, an Ownership Republic, and a democracy where nobody picks the policy. Two parties fight elections and voters decide.

Power: the transition (Geels)

An incumbent energy regime with lock-in (policy, industry, culture, technology aligned) against a niche technology that gets ~20% cheaper every time its installed base doubles. Landscape shocks crack the regime's alignment and open windows of opportunity. The niche needs a supply chain and installers to scale. Past 50% of capacity, it becomes the new regime.

Politics: the loop (Frey)

Democracy run only. Government picks policy, the economy responds, and voters judge growth, jobs, inflation and their own paychecks against what they've gotten used to. A safe incumbent pushes its ideology. An unpopular one buys votes before the election and pays for it afterward. Regime owners fund the status-quo story. Citizens who hold stakes vote to keep them.

How it works

  • Variation: firms spend on R&D (more innovation, higher price) and imitate the leader.
  • Selection: market share grows for firms with better productivity-to-price, a replicator rule from Nelson & Winter (1982).
  • Retention: dead firms are replaced by mutated copies of survivors.
  • Bottleneck: every firm buys a scarce input (energy, chips, magnets). Fewer owners means higher rent.
  • AI: automation shrinks labor's share. Owners of capital collect the difference.
  • Demand: the rich save more of each dollar. Concentrated ownership means weaker demand.
  • Power (Geels 2002): AI raises the energy needed per unit of output. No capacity, no output. The incumbent regime is cheap at first but locked in. The niche follows Wright's law. Shocks open windows. Capacity can only grow as fast as the build limit allows.
  • Politics (Frey 1978): two parties. The Incumbent Order protects the regime and routes the subsidy to the top. The Ownership Party routes it to citizen stakes, backs the niche, adds build capacity and slowly breaks up chokepoints. Elections every 4 years.
  • The one switch: the same asset subsidy goes to the top fifth (status quo) or into titled, universal stakes seeded heavier for the bottom half (Republic).

Falsifiers: what would prove this wrong

  • If spending out of capital income doesn't fall as wealth rises, the demand channel disappears, and with it the GDP gain.
  • If seeded accounts are consumed early instead of held, the equality gain collapses.
  • At 0% dividend the Republic is fairer but slower than the status quo. Locked money isn't spent. We found this in our own model and we're publishing it.
  • If incumbents with high popularity lost as often as unpopular ones, the evaluation loop is wrong and politics here is noise.
  • If the niche broke through just as fast without policy support, industrial policy is wasted money, and the Republic's power advantage is fake.
  • Automation-driven unemployment rises in both economies. Ownership at 1.4% of GDP spreads the gains. It does not, by itself, stop the job loss.
