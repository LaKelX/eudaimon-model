The firm landscape: evolution in real time

Each bubble is a firm. Right = more productive. Up = higher markup. Size = market share. Brighter = later generation (descendants of survivors). Firms that fall below 0.7% share die and are replaced by mutated copies of winners.
Press Run. The verdict updates as the years pass.

How it works

  • Variation: firms spend on R&D (more innovation, higher price) and imitate the leader.
  • Selection: market share grows for firms with better productivity-to-price, a replicator rule from Nelson & Winter (1982).
  • Retention: dead firms are replaced by mutated copies of survivors.
  • Bottleneck: every firm buys a scarce input (energy, chips, magnets). Fewer owners means higher rent.
  • AI: automation shrinks labor's share. Owners of capital collect the difference.
  • Demand: the rich save more of each dollar. Concentrated ownership means weaker demand.
  • The one switch: the same asset subsidy goes to the top fifth (status quo) or into titled, universal stakes seeded heavier for the bottom half (Republic).

Falsifiers: what would prove this wrong

  • If spending out of capital income doesn't fall as wealth rises, the demand channel disappears, and with it the GDP gain.
  • If seeded accounts are consumed early instead of held, the equality gain collapses.
  • At 0% dividend the Republic is fairer but slower than the status quo. Locked money isn't spent. We found this in our own model and we're publishing it.
  • Automation-driven unemployment rises in both economies. Ownership at 1.4% of GDP spreads the gains. It does not, by itself, stop the job loss.
